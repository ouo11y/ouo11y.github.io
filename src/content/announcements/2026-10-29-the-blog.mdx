The [blog](/blog/) is open, with twenty-two posts. Its rule: a post earns its place when you finish it able to do something new. Every command, config and number in a hands-on post was run or measured on a real setup before it was published, and the post says which versions it ran on. Most of it is about OpenTelemetry and open-source tools in general, not about ouo11y.

## Six series

| Series | Parts | What you come away with |
|---|---|---|
| [End-to-end observability, hands-on](/blog/series/end-to-end-observability-hands-on/) | 4 | one click followed through queues, databases and Redis, and exemplars from a metric to its trace |
| [Kubernetes observability without a vendor](/blog/series/kubernetes-observability-without-a-vendor/) | 3 | a blueprint, the collectors, and dashboards with a drill-down |
| [OpenTelemetry, hands-on](/blog/series/opentelemetry-hands-on/) | 3 | how OpenTelemetry came to be, OTTL v1 one statement at a time, declarative configuration on the Java agent |
| [SLOs from first principles](/blog/series/slos-from-first-principles/) | 4 | SLI, SLO, SLA and the error budget, choosing SLIs, burn-rate alerts, and a hands-on with Sloth, Prometheus and Grafana |
| [The math of observability](/blog/series/the-math-of-observability/) | 4 | nines and MTTR, percentiles and histograms, sampling, queues and Little's law, with calculators |
| [OpenTelemetry in the browser](/blog/series/opentelemetry-in-the-browser/) | 2 | how the Browser SIG works, and where OpenTelemetry in the browser stands in October 2026 |

Two single posts round it out: [RUM without our SDK](/blog/rum-without-our-sdk/), on what ouo11y needs from a browser, and [redacting once at the collector with OTTL](/blog/ottl-redaction-at-the-edge/).
